Machine learning in signal processing seeks interpretable representations from unstructured data. Raw signals lack semantics; thus, models must learn features capturing underlying structure for tasks. This thesis develops such methods accordingly. Chapter 3 addresses heteroscedastic noise where traditional subspace methods degrade. We propose ALPCAH, downweighting noisier samples to recover accurate subspaces without explicit quality knowledge. It outperforms PCA for scientific structure discovery. Chapter 4 extends this to union-of-subspaces settings by introducing ALPCAHUS, which is an ensemble clustering approach clustering samples under heteroscedastic noise while estimating multiple subspace bases simultaneously, improving clustering in heterogeneous data. Chapter 5 focuses on deep clustering with imbalanced clusters. We extend TURTLE by replacing its uniform cluster-size assumption with a power-law prior on cluster proportions. The resulting method, PET-TURTLE, improves performance in imbalanced regimes common in medical imaging. The remaining chapters deal with Alzheimer's disease and functional MRI. As brain atrophy precedes symptoms, learning aids early detection. Chapter 6 studies AD diagnosis using resting-state fMRI, proposing a novel 4D CNN on raw spatiotemporal data, improving diagnostics over traditional 3D models. MoCA, an exam for those suspect of early Alzheimers, detects cognitive impairment. Chapter 7 introduces NeuroMamba, a deep state space model predicting MoCA scores from resting-state fMRI. It uncovers brain-behavior links stronger than connectivity baselines, highlighting AD-related regions for interventions. Additionally, Chapter 7 presents preliminary results on MoCA prediction from task-based fMRI, including face-name and object-location association tasks.
